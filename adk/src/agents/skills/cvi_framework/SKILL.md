# CVI Framework Skill

This skill guides the generation of content following the Customer Value
Imperative (CVI) framework. It helps in structuring sales messages into two main
parts: **Why Change** and **Why You** (Why Google).

## Framework Structure

### Part 1: Why Change

This section focuses on building the case for change by helping the customer
recognize their current challenges and the implications of not addressing them.

**Target Audience & Context (Business Pitch):**

-   **Audience**: Line of Business (LOB) leaders, C-level executives, VPs &
    Directors of specific LOB.
-   **Sales Cycle / Win Cycle**: Stage 0-1.
-   **Goal of Presentation**:
    -   Get the prospect to understand why they should care/change.
    -   Secure a second meeting.
    -   Get buy-in for a deeper technical discussion.

#### 01 Current State & Problem Awareness

-   **Goal**: Establish the context and guide / articulate current challenges
    and pain points.
-   **Context**: Your first job isn’t to convince prospects to choose you over
    the competition—it’s to persuade them they need to change at all. Your
    biggest competitor is the status quo (40% of deals end in "no decision").
    The buyer is likely untroubled about the things you need them to be troubled
    about.
-   **Questions to Address**:
    -   **Trends & Changes**:
        -   What industry trends & shifts are putting pressure on your
            prospect’s desired outcomes?
        -   What risks do these trends pose?
        -   What concrete examples illustrate these trends?
    -   **Flawed Approach & Flawed Thinking**:
        -   How is your prospect responding to these pressures?
        -   Why are they taking this approach?
        -   What’s the problem they think they’re solving, or the underlying
            assumption driving that approach?
        -   Why is this approach not working?
    -   **Unconsidered Needs**:
        -   What needs or problems has the prospect not considered or
            underappreciated?
        -   How can you lead with these to defeat the status quo and avoid
            commoditized conversations?

#### 02 Implication & Business Impact

-   **Goal**: Deepen the understanding of the problem by connecting it to
    broader business consequences.
-   **Instructions**: Move a challenge to a critical business driver.
-   **Questions to Address**:
    -   **Consequence of No Change**:
        -   What will happen if they continue their current trajectory?
        -   What are the negative consequences to the business?
        -   What is keeping these companies not moving? What does their world
            look like vs other priorities?

#### 03 Future State & Desired Outcomes

-   **Goal**: Help envision a better future and articulate the value of solving
    the problem.
-   **Questions to Address**:
    -   **New Thinking** (Google related but not product specific):
        -   How *should* they be thinking about their current situation?
        -   What’s the *real* problem they should be focused on?
    -   **New Way** (Enterprise Cloud solution/product but impact driven):
        -   What approach should they adopt to solve their *real* problem (other
            than buying your solution)?
    -   **Likely Outcome (Goals)**:
        -   What business results should they expect to achieve by solving their
            *real* problem?

### Part 2: Why You (Why Google)

This section focuses on why the customer should choose Google to help them
achieve their desired future state.

**Target Audience & Context (Technical Pitch):**

-   **Audience**: CIO, CTO (Technical Decision Makers), IT Directors,
    Developers, Practitioners (implementation section in appendix).
-   **Sales Cycle / Win Cycle**: Stage 2.
-   **Goal of Presentation**:
    -   Prove technical viability.
    -   Build confidence for partnering on a Proof of Concept (POC).

#### 04 The Solution and Differentiation

-   **Goal**: Connect the solution to the problem, differentiate why Google, and
    prove results.
-   **Key Elements**:
    -   **Connect Solution to The Problem**:
        -   **Customer Challenge**: State the challenge using the pattern: "A
            challenge that we hear from a lot of customers is..."
        -   **Do Statement**: Frame the action using: "What if you could..."
            (Choose up to three unique capabilities).
    -   **Differentiate Why Google**:
        -   **Means Statement**: Frame the business impact using: "Which means
            you’ll see..."
        -   **Is Statement**: Frame the differentiator using: "And this is only
            available with..."
        -   **Telling Details**: Elaborate your differentiators with
            buyer-focused specifics.
    -   **Prove Results with Customer Examples**:
        -   **Before**: Describe the customer’s challenges before working with
            you.
        -   **After**: Present what they can now do differently because of your
            product/service/solution.
        -   **Impact**: Explain what this means for the customer by covering the
            positive impact on business outcomes.

## Usage Guidelines

1.  **Sequence**: Follow the sequence 01 -> 02 -> 03 -> 04 to build a logical
    and persuasive narrative.
2.  **Tone**: Empathetic to the customer's challenges, visionary about the
    future state, and confident about Google's capabilities.
3.  **Verifiability**: Ensure that the generated content clearly maps to these
    sections when requested.

## Gotchas

-   **Avoid mixing**: Do not mix "Why Change" and "Why You" messages on the same
    slide when building slides for GTM. Keep the narrative steps distinct to
    ensure clarity and impact.
